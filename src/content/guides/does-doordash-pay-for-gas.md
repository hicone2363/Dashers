## Quick Answer

No. DoorDash does not pay or reimburse gas as a separate item. Dashers cover fuel and maintenance themselves. What DoorDash offers are tools and perks: the **Crimson** debit card with cash back on gas (capped at $600 of gas spend per month, at the pump only) and a Gas Finder tool in the app. At tax time, you may be able to deduct vehicle costs.

---

## What DoorDash Does and Does Not Cover

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| **Gas reimbursement** | Not offered as a separate payment |
| **Mileage in pay** | Factored into per-offer base pay. In California, guaranteed pay adds a per-mile amount for active time only |
| **Gas Finder (GasBuddy)** | Available in the Dasher app |
| **Crimson card cash back on gas** | Available to approved cardholders, subject to monthly spend caps |
| **DasherDirect card** | Program ended on April 1, 2025 (replaced by DoorDash Crimson) |

## DoorDash Crimson: Gas Cash Back

- Cash back applies to gas purchased **at the pump** and to EV charging.
- Gas cash back is capped at a maximum of **$600 in total gas spend per calendar month**.
- Cash back percentages and promotional tier rates are displayed inside the Crimson Hub in the Dasher app and may change without notice. Check your in-app Hub for current promotional terms.
- DoorDash Crimson includes a checking account with a Visa business debit card. Couriers should review the official fee schedule and account agreement before opening an account.

## Tax Deduction for Gas and Mileage

Under federal tax rules, independent couriers can generally offset automobile operating expenses using one of two methods ([IRS Publication 463 / Topic 510](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-463)):

1. **Standard Mileage Rate:** Multiplying total business miles by the official federal rate.
2. **Actual Expenses:** Tracking all actual out-of-pocket receipts (gas, oil, insurance, tires, depreciation, repairs) and deducting the business-use percentage.

### Key IRS Rules on Choosing Your Method
- **The First-Year Rule:** If you want to use the standard mileage rate for a car you own, you must choose it in the first year the car is available for business use. In later years, you can choose either the standard mileage rate or actual expenses.
- **The Depreciation Consequence:** If you switch from standard mileage to actual expenses in a later year, IRS Publication 463 states that you cannot use accelerated MACRS depreciation. You must use straight-line depreciation over the estimated remaining useful life of the vehicle.
- **Leased Vehicles:** If you lease your delivery vehicle and choose the standard mileage rate, you must use the standard mileage rate method for the entire lease period (including renewals).

### 2026 IRS Standard Business Mileage Rates
- **72.5¢ per mile:** for miles driven from January 1 through June 30, 2026
- **76.0¢ per mile:** for miles driven from July 1 through December 31, 2026

**Illustrative Calculation Example (10,000 Annual Miles):**
*Assuming an illustrative 50/50 split of 5,000 miles driven in each half of the calendar year:*
- 5,000 delivery miles (Jan 1–Jun 30): $5,000 \times \$0.725 = \$3,625$.
- 5,000 delivery miles (Jul 1–Dec 31): $5,000 \times \$0.760 = \$3,800$.
- **Total Estimated Potential Deduction:** $\mathbf{\$7,425}$.

Always maintain a contemporaneous mileage log recording dates, destinations, and trip purposes. Consult a certified CPA or tax professional for your personal tax filing.
