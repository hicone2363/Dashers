## Quick Answer

DoorDash does not pay Dashers an hourly wage the way an employer pays staff. Dashers work as independent contractors, and most earn per offer. In select US cities, **Earn by Time** mode pays a minimum guaranteed rate for *active time only* (from accepting an offer to completing it), plus 100% of tips. A few places, including New York City, Seattle and California, also have local pay-floor laws.

---

## How Dashers Are Paid

DoorDash couriers are independent contractors (receiving a Form 1099 for tax reporting), not W-2 employees. Under DoorDash's [Independent Contractor Agreement](https://www.doordash.com/dasher/us/ica/), couriers choose when, where, and how much they deliver.

Dashers generally choose between two earning modes:
1. **[Earn per Offer (Pay Per Delivery)](/doordash-pay-per-delivery/):** The platform pays a set base fee for each accepted and completed delivery, plus 100% of tips and eligible promotions.
2. **[Earn by Time](/earn-by-time-doordash/):** The platform pays a guaranteed hourly rate strictly for active delivery minutes, plus 100% of tips.

---

## Earn by Time: The Closest Thing to Hourly

If you operate in an area where DoorDash offers Earn by Time, it functions as an active-time hourly guarantee rather than a standard job wage:

- Paid for **active time** only. Idle time between offers is not covered.
- Tips are paid 100% to you in addition to the guaranteed rate.
- Available in select cities only; the rate varies by location and time of day.

## Local Pay Laws (Verified October 24, 2026)

Because state and municipal regulations change periodically, couriers should always confirm current terms directly on the official municipal pages listed below.

### New York City
New York City sets a minimum pay rate for app-based restaurant and grocery delivery workers under Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) rules. The statutory rate is **$22.13 per hour before tips**, effective the first pay period on or after April 1, 2026, and is adjusted annually for inflation. 

Key regulatory provisions:
- Customer tips cannot be counted by platforms toward satisfying the minimum pay requirement.
- The rule protects app delivery workers across food and grocery platforms.

*Source: NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP).*

### Seattle
Under the Seattle Office of Labor Standards (OLS) App-Based Worker Minimum Payment Ordinance, platforms must pay the greater of:
- **(a) $0.47 per engaged minute + $0.80 per engaged mile**, or
- **(b) A $5.34 minimum per offer.**

These statutory rates are adjusted annually every January 1.

*Source: Seattle Office of Labor Standards (OLS).*

### California (Prop 22)
California's Proposition 22 guarantees app-based couriers a compensation floor based on **120% of the applicable local minimum wage for engaged time**, plus a per-mile reimbursement rate for engaged miles. 

Because California minimum wages differ across cities and counties based on pickup locations, this guide does not quote a single flat dollar rate. DoorDash calculates and reconciles payments on a bi-weekly basis.

*Source: DoorDash Help Center, "Your Prop 22 Guide."*

## How to Calculate Your Real Hourly Pay

When assessing whether your deliveries are profitable, looking only at the active-hour rate can be misleading. Always distinguish between gross time and net profit:

```text
True Gross Hourly = Total Pay (including tips) ÷ Total Dash Hours (including idle waiting)
```

```text
Real Net Hourly = [Total Pay − (Fuel Expenses + Vehicle Wear)] ÷ Total Dash Hours
```

## Don't Forget Mileage for Taxes

DoorDash advises Dashers—including those covered by California Prop 22—to maintain comprehensive records of all business mileage. 

Federal tax write-offs may cover total driving miles (including return trips), whereas state wage minimums only compensate for engaged delivery miles. 

The standard IRS business mileage deduction rate is:
- **72.5¢ per mile** (January 1 to June 30, 2026)
- **76.0¢ per mile** (July 1 to December 31, 2026)

*Always consult a qualified CPA or tax professional regarding your individual deductions.*
