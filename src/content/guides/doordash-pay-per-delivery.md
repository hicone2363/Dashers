## Quick Answer

In DoorDash's standard **Earn per Offer** mode (pay per delivery), you are paid a fixed amount for each delivery completed. Your total payout equals **Base Pay + Customer Tip + Promotions (Peak Pay)**. 

Base pay ranges from **$2 to $10+** depending on estimated trip time, distance, and order desirability. Base pay never decreases because of a customer tip, and you keep 100% of tips.

---

## The Three Components of DoorDash Delivery Pay

DoorDash officially defines per-offer compensation through three distinct elements:

```text
Total Delivery Earnings = Base Pay ($2–$10+) + Customer Tip (100%) + Promotions (when available)
```

### 1. Base Pay ($2.00 to $10.00+)
Base pay is DoorDash's platform contribution for the delivery. DoorDash states that base pay is calculated using three primary criteria:
- **Estimated Time:** How long the order is expected to take from pickup to drop-off.
- **Distance:** The total mileage between the merchant and the delivery destination.
- **Desirability:** A factor officially listed by DoorDash reflecting order characteristics and courier demand. DoorDash does not publicly disclose the specific algorithmic weights.

> **Tip Independence:** DoorDash's official policy explicitly confirms that **base pay does not change based on customer tips**. Whether a customer tips $0 or $20, the platform's base pay calculation remains independent.

### 2. Customer Tips
- Dashers receive **100% of customer tips**.
- In Earn per Offer, the offer acceptance screen displays the guaranteed minimum upfront payout, which often includes customer tips.
- Customers may tip during checkout or add tips after the delivery is completed.

### 3. Promotions (Peak Pay & Challenges)
- During periods of high customer demand or bad weather, DoorDash may add **Peak Pay** (such as an extra dollar amount per eligible delivery). Other incentives (such as delivery Challenges) may also be offered when available.
- Promotional pay is added on top of the standard base pay and customer tip.

## How the Offer Screen Works Before You Accept

When an offer appears on your device in Earn per Offer mode, the screen displays:
1. **Guaranteed Minimum Pay:** The total dollar amount you will earn upon completing the delivery (e.g., "$7.50").
2. **Total Mileage:** The driving distance from your current location to the store, and from the store to the customer.
3. **Merchant Name and Drop-Off Pin:** Showing where you pick up and where you deliver.
4. **Number of Items / Store Details:** Giving context on the size of the order.

You have a limited countdown timer (typically 35–45 seconds) to review the offer and decide whether to accept or decline.

## Acceptance Rate vs. Completion Rate: What Official Policies Say

Many couriers worry about declining low-paying delivery offers. It is critical to distinguish between **Acceptance Rate** and **Completion Rate**:

| Metric | What It Measures | Minimum for Deactivation? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acceptance Rate (AR)** | Percentage of incoming offers you choose to accept | **NO minimum.** You cannot be deactivated for a low acceptance rate. |
| **Completion Rate (CR)** | Percentage of accepted orders you actually finish delivering | **90% minimum.** Dropping below 90% can lead to account deactivation. |

*Sources: DoorDash Dasher Ratings Explained & Deactivation Policy. DoorDash independent contractor agreements affirm couriers have the contractual freedom to accept or reject any offer.*

## Community Rules of Thumb vs. Official Policy

Because DoorDash pays per delivery rather than reimbursing vehicle expenses, experienced drivers often use mathematical benchmarks before accepting an order. 

**These are independent courier strategies, NOT DoorDash policies:**

### 1. The Dollar-Per-Mile Rule
- **The Concept:** Many couriers target at least **$1.50 to $2.00 per total mile** shown on the offer card.
- **Why Drivers Use It:** Automotive expenses (gas at ~$3.45/gal and wear/maintenance at ~$0.15/mi) consume roughly $0.25 to $0.35 per mile. Offers below $1.00/mile often yield negligible net profit.

### 2. Factoring in Return Driving (Deadhead Miles)
- **The Concept:** If an offer pays $8.00 for 6 miles, but takes you out into a residential zone where no restaurants exist, you must drive 6 miles back.
- **The Reality:** That delivery is effectively a 12-mile round trip ($0.66/mile), dramatically reducing hourly earnings.

### 3. Time Efficiency
- A $6.00 delivery completed in 12 minutes equals a **$30.00/hr gross pace**.
- A $10.00 delivery that requires waiting 25 minutes at a slow merchant plus 15 minutes of driving equals a **$15.00/hr gross pace**.

## Earn per Offer vs. Earn by Time: Summary

| Feature | Earn per Offer (Pay Per Delivery) | Earn by Time (Hourly Active) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Payment Structure** | Fixed fee per delivery + tip | Guaranteed hourly rate for active minutes + tip |
| **Declining Flexibility** | Unlimited declines (no deactivation risk) | Maximum 1 decline per hour |
| **Restaurant Wait Time** | Unpaid (included in flat base pay) | Paid (accrues active hourly rate) |
| **Order Transparency** | Full upfront payout and distance shown | Mileage and drop-off time shown |
